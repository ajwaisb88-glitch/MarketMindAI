It all rides on one number: is the edge real?

The signal has to actually predict the next tick. A coin-flip loses to fees; below ~57% you bleed out.

The hidden killer: leverage & fees

Tighter scalps need more leverage — and the fee is charged on the leveraged size. Chasing 0.2% moves quietly bankrupts most accounts.

How the strategy is wired

01 · SIGNAL

Manipulation radar

Order-book imbalance, cancel/trade ratio and Hawkes cancel-bursts fuse via Bayes into a 0–100 spoof score. A detected spoof wall points the scalp direction.

02 · SIZE

Fractional Kelly

Stake = ½·(bp−q)/b of equity, capped at 5%. No edge → Kelly says stake nothing, so the bot simply doesn't trade.

03 · EXIT

Volatility bracket

Symmetric take-profit / stop sized to recent quantile volatility. Every trade pays a round-trip fee on the leveraged notional — win or lose.

Verdict

The 10× is real but rare. Even granting an optimistic, durable 58% win rate, only about 1 in 80 accounts reaches $1,000 over 600 scalps. The median account ends near $128, and ~2 in 5 finish underwater. A "live $100 → $1,000" run is the winning line out of many — the losing lines don't get posted.

The edge is the whole game. Drop the win rate to a coin-flip and fees turn it into a slow bleed; push leverage up to chase tighter scalps and ruin probability explodes (86% at 0.2% targets). The strategy can compound a genuine edge — but it manufactures nothing where no edge exists.

Educational backtest on simulated microstructure data, not live-market results and not financial advice. The win rate is a modelled input — the honest, unprovable assumption in any such claim. Reproduce with python -m app.scalping in the MarketMind AI repo.
